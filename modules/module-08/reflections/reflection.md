# Reflection — Module 08: Deep Learning – Big Data and Architectures

## A08 / L08 — Fact or Fiction: Understanding GANs Through Story

**What challenged me this module:**
Writing a "mixed" story (Story C) that was genuinely hard to classify
required understanding real GAN history well enough to know which facts
were true (Goodfellow's Montreal-bar origin story is real) before
deciding which specifics to fabricate around them (the MNIST accuracy
figure, the NeurIPS standing ovation, the hospital deployment). You can't
write convincing misinformation about a topic you don't actually
understand.

**What clicked that didn't before:**
The most believable story wasn't the most dramatic one — it was the one
with small, specific, low-stakes, hard-to-fabricate details. That's
backwards from what I expected going in; I assumed convincing fiction
would need to be compelling, when it actually needed to be mundane and
textured instead.

**How I'd explain this concept to someone who knows nothing about AI:**
A GAN is two AI systems arguing with each other — one makes fakes, the
other tries to catch them, and both get better from the argument. We did
the human version: half the team wrote true stories, half wrote made-up
ones, and everyone else had to guess which was which. The version that
blended a real fact with fake details fooled the most people — that's
exactly how a well-designed fake evades detection.

**One thing I want to go deeper on:**
Whether the specific signal our human evaluators used (suspiciously
precise numbers, tonal shifts from technical to emotional language) maps
onto anything a real trained Discriminator network actually looks at, or
whether human and machine pattern-detection diverge more than the
activity's framing suggests.

**How this connects to the broader AI landscape:**
This is a direct, hands-on version of the deepfake/synthetic-media
problem — Story C's success (real facts as camouflage for fabrication)
is structurally the same technique that makes GAN-generated
disinformation dangerous at scale. Understanding why the mixed story
fooled people is understanding why partial truth is more dangerous than
outright fabrication.
