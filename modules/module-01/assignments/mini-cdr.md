# Mini CDR — Module 01: AI History Research Paper (A01)

```
Mini CDR — Module 01: AI History Research Paper (A01)
|
├── Problem / Prompt
|   └── Write a research paper tracing AI from its conceptual roots to the
|       present — historical milestones, influential figures, ethical
|       impact per era, and future directions, with proper citations.
|
├── Approach
|   └── Structured the paper around four phases of development (early
|       concepts → Turing/Dartmouth → symbolic AI/winters → deep
|       learning/LLMs), and picked one throughline — symbolic rules vs.
|       learned patterns — to argue instead of just listing events
|       chronologically.
|
├── What worked
|   ├── Anchoring every section to the central tension (rules vs. learning)
|   |   kept the paper from turning into a disconnected timeline.
|   └── Pairing each era with its own ethical concern (accountability in
|       expert systems → bias in ML → surveillance/labor in the modern
|       era) made the ethics section feel earned, not bolted on.
|
├── What didn't / had to change
|   └── First pass leaned too heavily on dates and names without
|       connecting them — had to go back and tie every figure's
|       contribution to the central argument, not just "here's what
|       they did."
|
├── What I'd do differently next time
|   └── Spend more time up front deciding the thesis before drafting,
|       rather than finding it partway through writing.
|
└── Key concept takeaway
    └── The "AI effect" — intelligence gets redefined as whatever
        machines still can't do — explains why AI always feels both
        overhyped and perpetually just-out-of-reach at the same time.
```
