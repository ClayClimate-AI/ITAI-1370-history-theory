**What challenged me this module:**
Deciding what level of granularity to apply to the secondary nodes.
Bourke's roadmap is extensive, and collapsing it into a single knowledge
graph meant deliberate choices about which subtopics mattered enough to
represent versus which would just create visual clutter. A second challenge
was keeping the cross-branch dashed connector lines legible without them
overlapping.

**What clicked that didn't before:**
Before this exercise I understood most of these terms individually but
treated them as a checklist to learn in sequence. Mapping them visually made
clear they're not sequential at all — they're an ecosystem. Python doesn't
just "lead to" machine learning; it runs through every branch of the graph
simultaneously.

**How I'd explain this concept to someone who knows nothing about AI:**
NLP and Computer Vision aren't endpoints you arrive at after learning
enough — they're where Techniques and Learning Approaches actually converge
into something real. And Tools and Frameworks like cloud platforms and
version control aren't a stage you pass through once — they're the
connective tissue every other part of the map depends on the whole time.

**One thing I want to go deeper on:**
How the graph would need to be redrawn once you add newer areas like
foundation models / LLMs as their own branch — do they sit under Techniques
alongside Neural Networks, or do they deserve their own primary node given
how much of the current field runs through them?

**How this connects to the broader AI landscape:**
This realization will inform how I prioritize my own learning going
forward — recognizing which skills are infrastructure (always relevant) vs.
which are technique-specific (relevant to one branch) changes what's worth
investing in early.
