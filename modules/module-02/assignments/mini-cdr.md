## A02 — Why Google Was Created / AI Patents (2014–2023)

```
Mini CDR — A02: Why Google Was Created / AI Patents
|
├── Problem / Prompt
|   └── Two-part submission: explain why Google was created (the search
|       problem, PageRank as the solution), then analyze AI patent filing
|       trends 2014-2023 using WIPO's GenAI patent landscape data.
|
├── Approach
|   └── Structured Part 1 around problem → solution → mission (keyword
|       stuffing was gameable → link-based ranking → "organize the
|       world's information"), then let Part 2's patent-surge data speak
|       through four dated milestones rather than a vague growth trend.
|
├── What worked
|   └── Anchoring the patent surge to four concrete dated events (2017
|       Transformer, 2020 GPT-3, 2022 ChatGPT, 2023 record year) instead
|       of describing "rapid AI growth" abstractly — each milestone has a
|       specific, citable trigger.
|
├── What didn't / had to change
|   └── n/a — straightforward two-part research report.
|
├── What I'd do differently next time
|   └── Pull in a comparison of which companies/countries are actually
|       filing (the China-vs-US split is mentioned once) rather than just
|       the aggregate yearly count.
|
└── Key concept takeaway
    └── Founding technical insights (PageRank, the Transformer) don't just
        launch one product — they trigger an entire industry's downstream
        patent activity for years afterward.
```

## L02 — Key People & Foundational Work in AI History

```
Mini CDR — L02: Key People & Foundational Work in AI History
|
├── Problem / Prompt
|   └── Build a research spreadsheet documenting 11 foundational AI
|       figures — for each: best date of work, institution, key papers,
|       funding source, final product, today's derived capability, and
|       where that work continues today.
|
├── Approach
|   └── Traced every figure all the way from their original landmark work
|       to a named system in use today (e.g., Rosenblatt's 1958 Perceptron
|       → "direct ancestor of every deep learning layer"), instead of
|       stopping at the historical fact.
|
├── What worked
|   └── Documenting the funding source column for every person surfaced a
|       pattern I wasn't looking for: nearly every foundational AI
|       breakthrough was funded by DARPA, ONR, NSF, or internal corporate
|       R&D (Bell Labs, IBM) — not neutral academic curiosity.
|
├── What didn't / had to change
|   └── n/a — research/documentation task, no revision needed.
|
├── What I'd do differently next time
|   └── Add a 12th row for a non-Western AI research figure/institution —
|       the current list is entirely US/Canada/France-based despite AI
|       research (and, per A02, AI patent filing) being a global effort.
|
└── Key concept takeaway
    └── AI history isn't a sequence of disconnected breakthroughs — it's
        one continuous, traceable lineage. Every row in this spreadsheet
        connects to a system I use in this course (Claude, GPT-4, Gemini)
        by name, not by vague resemblance.
```

## Puzzle 02 — Google's Hat Puzzle

```
Mini CDR — Puzzle 02: Google's Hat Puzzle
|
├── Problem / Prompt
|   └── Solve the 100-prisoner hat puzzle (guarantee the maximum number of
|       survivors using a pre-agreed strategy, no communication once the
|       game starts), then connect the solution to core AI concepts.
|
├── Approach
|   └── Worked the parity strategy interactively — built a step-by-step
|       deduction simulator and a full logic tree to walk through the
|       reasoning person by person instead of just accepting the known
|       solution as given.
|
├── What worked
|   ├── Building the 10-person worked example (concrete hats, concrete
|   |   calls) is what actually made the abstract "starting parity − reds
|   |   called − reds ahead" formula click.
|   └── Mapping each puzzle mechanic to a named AI concept (parity → error
|       correction, red/blue call → binary classification, updating
|       belief on each call → Bayesian inference) turned a math riddle
|       into a real study of reasoning under incomplete information.
|
├── What didn't / had to change
|   └── Initial read of the puzzle assumed it was unsolvable because no
|       one can see their own hat — had to abandon "solve it per-person"
|       and reframe as "solve it as a group system" before the strategy
|       made sense.
|
├── What I'd do differently next time
|   └── Try the "More Colours" (3+ hat colors) extension by hand before
|       reading the multi-class-classification mapping, to see if I'd
|       derive the additional-bit-per-color logic myself.
|
└── Key concept takeaway
    └── One bit of well-chosen shared information (parity) is enough to
        turn a group of individually-blind agents into a system with 99%
        guaranteed accuracy — the rule is the intelligence, not any
        single agent's knowledge.
```
